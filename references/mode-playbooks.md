# Mode Playbooks

Load when the target mode is unfamiliar, or when the corpus mode and the target mode differ enough that direct transfer would misfire.

Each playbook says what to carry, what to suppress, and what the genre demands regardless of voice.

---

## 회고·서사

**Carry:** everything. This is the mode where a personal voice is least constrained.

**Suppress:** nothing structural. Only correct mechanics.

**Genre demands:** a scene the reader can locate; movement in time; a reason the memory is being told now. Restraint at the ending — the genre punishes summing up the meaning out loud.

**Common failure:** the drafter adds a reflective closing paragraph the writer would never have written. If the corpus keeps emotion implicit, stop one paragraph earlier than feels natural.

---

## 설명·분석

**Carry:** concreteness, information order, sentence rhythm, certainty level.

**Suppress:** metaphor density (halve it), narrative digression, delayed thesis.

**Genre demands:** define terms before using them; make causality explicit; separate what is measured from what is inferred; let the reader skim via structure.

**Common failure:** hedging collapses. A writer who says "~인 듯하다" in essays often has that flattened into flat assertion here. Preserve the hedge — it is factual integrity, not decoration.

---

## 설득·제안

**Carry:** certainty level, diction, rhythm, the writer's characteristic evidence type (anecdote? number? analogy?).

**Suppress:** ambiguity about the ask. Ornamental openings.

**Genre demands:** thesis in the first paragraph; the reader's objection acknowledged before it is raised; a specific, actionable request at the end.

**Common failure:** voice traits are kept that actively weaken the case — long qualifying subordinate clauses, buried claims. Keep only traits that survive contact with a skeptical reader.

---

## 공식·사업

**Carry:** concreteness, information order, and the writer's habit of specific evidence. These make formal documents better, not worse.

**Suppress:** metaphor, humor, conversational asides, long sentences, 해요체, direct address.

**Genre demands:** scanability (headings, short paragraphs, tables); numbers with sources; explicit scope and limits; a fixed speech level, usually 합니다체 or 명사형 개조식 depending on the document.

**Common failure:** the drafter either flattens the writer into template prose, or preserves narrative habits that read as unserious to a reviewer. The correct target is a document that a 심사위원 reads as professional but that still sounds like a specific person wrote it — usually achieved through *what is chosen as evidence*, not through sentence style.

---

## 커뮤니티·홍보

**Carry:** naturalness, specificity, humor, direct address, the writer's actual vocabulary.

**Suppress:** long setup, formal connectives, anything that reads as marketing copy.

**Genre demands:** the point in the first two lines; a concrete reason the reader should care; no superlatives the writer cannot back; platform norms (Reddit dislikes announcements, LinkedIn tolerates them, X rewards compression).

**Common failure:** hype. If the corpus never uses 혁신적, 최고의, 완벽한, the draft must not either — even when the subject is the writer's own product. Under-claiming reads as credible; over-claiming reads as generated.

## Cross-Mode Transfer Table

When the corpus is mostly mode A and the target is mode B, this is the rough conversion.

| From → To | Sentence length | Metaphor | Speech level | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 회고·서사 → 설명·분석 | −25% | −50% | 한다체 or 합니다체 | thesis earlier |
| 회고·서사 → 공식·사업 | −40% | −90% | 합니다체 / 개조식 | headings, evidence-first |
| 회고·서사 → 커뮤니티·홍보 | −30% | keep | 해요체 or 반말 | point first |
| 설명·분석 → 설득·제안 | keep | keep | keep | claim first, ask last |
| 설명·분석 → 공식·사업 | −15% | −70% | 합니다체 | tables, scope statement |
| any → any | — | — | — | certainty level never changes |

The last row is the rule that matters most. Sentence length, imagery, and formality are all negotiable by genre. How sure the writer sounds is not.
